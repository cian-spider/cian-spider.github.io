Assumptions

  • Two readings of 'the cycle where control shifted' are defensible, so both are returned. Primary answer: the 2014 midterms, the last cycle in which control of the Senate changed hands to the Republicans (net +9, 54-46 majority in the 114th Congress).
  • Secondary answer: the 2020 general election, the most recent cycle in which Senate control changed at all (to the Democrats, via the 50-50 tie and the Vice President's casting vote). In that cycle exactly one seat flipped to a Republican, Alabama.
  • Incumbent status is taken from the seat holder immediately before the election; open seats are labelled as such because the Democratic incumbent retired.
  • Vote totals and percentages are the certified general-election (or runoff, for Louisiana) results as published in the cited article; margins are computed as winner minus runner-up, not copied.
  • Third-party candidates are excluded from the margin, so margin_pct can be smaller than 100 minus the loser's share.
